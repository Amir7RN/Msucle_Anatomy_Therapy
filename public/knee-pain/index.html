Which muscle is causing my knee pain?

Knee pain often starts in the muscles that move and stabilise the knee — not the joint surface itself. Here are the muscles that most commonly cause knee pain and how to confirm your source on a 3D body model.

Muscles that commonly cause knee pain

  • Quadriceps (esp. VMO) — Front-of-knee and kneecap pain, worse on stairs or squats; weak inner-quad (VMO) lets the kneecap track poorly.
  • IT band / tensor fasciae latae (TFL) — Sharp outer-knee pain, common in runners, that flares with repeated bending.
  • Hamstrings — Back-of-knee tightness and pain; tight hamstrings load the knee and limit extension.
  • Popliteus — Deep back-of-knee pain, especially when unlocking the knee or going downhill.
  • Gastrocnemius (calf) — Calf tightness that pulls behind the knee and affects how you push off.

Why does the front of my knee hurt on stairs?

Front-of-knee pain on stairs often relates to quadriceps (VMO) weakness and kneecap tracking. Zevahealth AI helps you target the right muscle to offload the knee.

When should I see a doctor for knee pain?

Seek care for knee pain after injury, with swelling, locking, giving way, or inability to bear weight. Zevahealth AI offers general movement guidance, not diagnosis.
