Which muscle is causing my shoulder pain?

Shoulder pain rarely comes from the joint alone. More often a specific muscle — strained, overworked, or holding tension — is the real source. Muscles that commonly cause shoulder pain

  • Anterior deltoid — Front-of-shoulder pain that flares when you lift your arm forward or press overhead. Common in lifters and after repetitive reaching.
  • Supraspinatus (rotator cuff) — A deep ache on the outer/top shoulder when raising the arm to the side, often worst between 60–120°. A frequent cause of "can't reach overhead" pain.
  • Infraspinatus & teres minor — Back-of-shoulder pain and weakness when rotating the arm outward; can refer pain down the arm.
  • Upper trapezius — Pain across the top of the shoulder and into the neck, tied to desk posture, stress, and carrying loads.
  • Levator scapulae — Pain where the neck meets the shoulder blade, worse after long screen time or sleeping awkwardly.
  • Pectoralis minor — Front-shoulder tightness that pulls the shoulder forward; linked to rounded posture and can mimic impingement.
